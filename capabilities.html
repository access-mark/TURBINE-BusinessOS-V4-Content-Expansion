CAPABILITY LANDSCAPE

01 / 12

Customers, contacts and CRM

Maintain a shared view of organisations, people, relationships and commercial activity. Connect customer context to quotations, commitments, service work and subsequent financial activity instead of rebuilding a customer history across multiple applications.

What this capability means

The customer record is the commercial memory of the business. It should describe who the customer is, who may act for them, what has been promised, and what remains outstanding.

Operational scope

Maintain customer and organisation profiles, contacts, service addresses, responsible account owners and relationship histories. Distinguish prospects from active customers without losing the original enquiry or duplicating records.

A practical business journey

An enquiry becomes a qualified opportunity; the responsible person records requirements, schedules a follow-up and prepares a quotation. When accepted, the same customer context travels into delivery, invoicing and account review.

How different businesses use it

A sole proprietor can see the entire customer relationship without maintaining separate spreadsheets. A growing IT services firm can give sales, service and finance a consistent customer context while controlling who may change commercial terms.

Management questions and controls

Review inactive opportunities, overdue follow-ups, concentration of revenue by customer and the relationship between accepted work and outstanding balances.

02 / 12

Sales, quotations and acceptance

Prepare structured quotations, preserve version history and support controlled acceptance. Commercial decisions should be traceable to the precise scope and price approved, reducing disputes caused by outdated documents or informal changes.

What this capability means

A quotation is a controlled commercial commitment, not simply a PDF sent to a customer. Scope, pricing, exclusions, taxes, approvals and acceptance must remain understandable throughout delivery.

Operational scope

Record the opportunity, proposed goods or services, quantities, pricing assumptions, validity dates and versions. Preserve the difference between a draft, a submitted quotation and an accepted commitment.

A practical business journey

A customer requests twenty workstations. The team captures the specification, obtains internal price approval, sends a versioned quotation and records acceptance. Fulfilment works from the accepted scope, not from a later informal email.

How different businesses use it

A consultant can quote a fixed-fee engagement; a distributor can quote stock and delivery; a contractor can separate materials, labour and milestones. Each uses the same underlying commercial discipline.

Management questions and controls

Compare quotation conversion, approval delays, margin assumptions, expired offers and changes made after customer acceptance.

03 / 12

Work, projects and scheduling

Coordinate tasks, appointments, resources and delivery obligations. Keep customer commitments visible alongside the operational work required to fulfil them, with clear ownership and status.

What this capability means

Accepted business must become accountable execution. Work should be visible as commitments, owners, due dates, dependencies and evidence of completion.

Operational scope

Structure jobs, projects, tasks, milestones, schedules and responsibilities. Relate the work to the customer commitment and surface exceptions when deadlines or resources are at risk.

A practical business journey

After a proposal is accepted, the delivery lead establishes milestones, assigns work and tracks progress. A change request is reviewed against the original scope before additional effort or billing is authorised.

How different businesses use it

A freelancer may manage a handful of deliverables; an agency may coordinate campaigns across specialists; an engineering firm may track procurement, site work and handover.

Management questions and controls

Review work in progress, overdue milestones, resource bottlenecks, unbilled completed work and deviations from agreed scope.

04 / 12

Procurement and supplier operations

Follow purchasing from demand and approval to order, goods receipt and supplier processing. Distinguish what was requested, what was delivered and what remains financially unresolved.

What this capability means

Purchasing should be governed by real demand and an accountable approval trail. Ordering, receipt and supplier settlement are distinct business events.

Operational scope

Maintain supplier records, purchase requests, approval responsibilities, purchase orders, receipts and discrepancies. Preserve links between the original requirement, the approved order and the invoice received.

A practical business journey

A project needs materials. The responsible person raises a request, an authorised approver releases the order, goods are received against the order and any short delivery is investigated before payment.

How different businesses use it

A small services firm may purchase occasional equipment; a manufacturer may purchase recurring inputs; a field-service business may order job-specific replacement parts.

Management questions and controls

Examine outstanding orders, unreceived items, supplier lead times, purchasing exceptions and commitments not yet reflected in cash outflow.

05 / 12

Inventory, stock and cost

Understand stock movements, availability, purchasing and cost-of-goods-sold relationships. For product-led businesses, inventory decisions should connect to both fulfilment and financial context.

What this capability means

Stock is both an operational resource and a financial commitment. Businesses need to know what is available, where it is, what it cost and why it moved.

Operational scope

Maintain product and item records, stock locations, receipts, issues, adjustments and cost relationships. Connect stock consumption to sales or work where the operating model requires it.

A practical business journey

A retailer receives goods against a purchase order, updates available stock and fulfils an order. A technician consumes a part on a service job, making the cost visible in that job rather than disappearing into a general expense.

How different businesses use it

A consultancy may need little inventory; a distributor depends on stock accuracy; a maintenance contractor needs visibility into vehicle or depot parts. Configuration should reflect the business without turning TURBINE into separate products.

Management questions and controls

Track stock-outs, slow-moving items, shrinkage, unfulfilled orders and the relationship between product margin and actual purchasing cost.

06 / 12

Finance, billing and receivables

Connect invoicing, customer balances, receipts and relevant accounting controls to the underlying work. The objective is better financial visibility with preserved transaction history, not a disconnected finance screen.

What this capability means

Financial records should explain the underlying business activity. An invoice, receipt or balance is more useful when it can be traced to the work or goods that generated it.

Operational scope

Manage billing events, invoice records, credit adjustments, customer balances, receipts and relevant accounting classifications. Preserve transaction history and reconciliation evidence.

A practical business journey

A completed project milestone triggers a billing review; the invoice refers to the accepted scope; a receipt is matched to the correct customer obligation; unresolved amounts remain visible for collection.

How different businesses use it

A sole proprietor needs a clear view of invoices and expenses. A professional-services firm needs milestone billing. A product-led business needs financial visibility over fulfilment and cost.

Management questions and controls

Review aged receivables, disputed invoices, cash collection, billing delays and the difference between revenue recorded and cash received. Statutory and accounting claims require qualification.

07 / 12

Banking interfaces and financial controls

TURBINE is intended to be the primary financial operating environment. Where external banking, payment or statutory interfaces are essential, they require consent, reconciliation and independent qualification. Connecting third-party business applications is not the product proposition.

What this capability means

TURBINE is intended to be the financial operating environment; banking rails and payment networks remain external infrastructure. Their use must not turn the proposition into an integration marketplace.

Operational scope

Define controlled imports or interfaces for bank activity, payment confirmations, reconciliations and authorised adjustments. Record source, date, amount, reference and reconciliation outcome.

A practical business journey

A payment arrives in the bank. The business matches it to an outstanding invoice, investigates ambiguous references and preserves evidence of who approved any manual correction.

How different businesses use it

Small businesses need confidence in cash position; larger businesses need separation of duties and exception handling. Both require reliable records rather than manual reconciliation in multiple spreadsheets.

Management questions and controls

Surface unmatched transactions, stale reconciliations, unexpected cash movements and approvals that exceed delegated authority.

08 / 12

Reporting and management visibility

Bring operational and financial information into meaningful management views. A useful report should explain which records drive a result, where attention is needed and which figures remain uncertain.

What this capability means

Management should be able to act on the same operational and financial records used by staff, rather than reconcile competing departmental reports.

Operational scope

Provide views of pipeline, work, purchasing, stock, billing, collections and operational exceptions. Explain the source and freshness of figures and distinguish estimates from posted transactions.

A practical business journey

A business owner starts the day with delayed projects, quotations awaiting action and overdue customer balances. Each indicator leads to the underlying record and responsible person.

How different businesses use it

A sole proprietor needs a concise daily picture; a growing SME needs team and departmental views; a multi-location operator needs appropriate organisational boundaries.

Management questions and controls

Prioritise actionable exceptions, cash conversion, delivery performance, margin drivers and the reliability of underlying records.

09 / 12

Turbine Guide and contextual intelligence

Help people find the right screen, understand a workflow and identify the next relevant action. Predictive and generative features require separate verification before being offered as production capabilities.

What this capability means

A comprehensive BusinessOS must remain understandable. Guidance belongs within the workflow at the point of decision, not in a separate manual that staff rarely open.

Operational scope

Explain terminology, show next steps, help users find functions and present contextual assistance based on role and current task. Advanced intelligence requires explicit qualification.

A practical business journey

A new employee preparing a first quotation sees the required sequence, mandatory information and approval path. A manager reviewing a blocked task sees why it cannot progress and which action is needed.

How different businesses use it

Sole proprietors benefit from guided setup and everyday administration; growing teams benefit from consistent processes and reduced dependence on informal training.

Management questions and controls

Evaluate task completion, error rates, unresolved guidance requests and the clarity of explanations. Do not present unverified autonomous decision-making as a released feature.

10 / 12

DataPort Import / Export Studio

A governed approach to importing and exporting business records at scale: templates, field mapping, validation, error handling and portability. Customer, contact, opportunity, accounting and transaction migrations require mapping and reconciliation before production cutover.

What this capability means

Full adoption requires a credible way to bring business history into TURBINE and retain data portability. Migration is a governed transition, not an indefinite connection to a fragmented software stack.

Operational scope

Provide import templates, field mapping, validation, duplicate handling, exception review, retry and export facilities for supported business records. Financial migrations require careful opening-balance and reconciliation controls.

A practical business journey

A company exports customers, contacts, opportunities and opening financial records from its previous tools. DataPort maps fields, identifies invalid rows, supports correction and produces a reconciliation record before cutover.

How different businesses use it

A sole proprietor may import a few hundred contacts; an established SME may migrate thousands of customers, suppliers, invoices and transactions in controlled batches.

Management questions and controls

Measure accepted and rejected rows, duplicate rates, unresolved exceptions, balance reconciliation and completeness of the signed-off cutover dataset.

11 / 12

Communication and collaboration

Keep important conversations, decisions and handoffs associated with the relevant business records. The intended model is contextual communication, not another isolated inbox.

What this capability means

Important conversations are part of business execution. Decisions and handoffs should remain associated with the relevant customer, work item or commercial record.

Operational scope

Record contextual notes, responsibilities, handoffs, notifications and decisions within the BusinessOS. Keep a clear distinction between internal discussion and approved external commitments.

A practical business journey

A sales colleague records a customer clarification against the opportunity; delivery sees the agreed change; finance sees the approved billing consequence. The decision is not lost in an unrelated chat thread.

How different businesses use it

A small team can preserve institutional knowledge as it grows; larger teams can coordinate sales, delivery and finance without repeatedly asking for the same background.

Management questions and controls

Review unanswered handoffs, pending approvals, commitments without owners and decisions made outside the controlled process.

12 / 12

Access, permissions and audit

Businesses need controlled user access, tenant separation, appropriate permissions and traceable changes. These controls are qualification requirements, not assumptions based on an attractive interface.

What this capability means

A single operating environment must still enforce boundaries. A connected business record does not mean every employee may see or change every field.

Operational scope

Define identities, roles, permissions, tenant isolation, approval authorities and audit events. Protect sensitive commercial and financial actions and preserve evidence for investigation and recovery.

A practical business journey

A salesperson may create a draft quotation but cannot approve an exceptional discount; an accounts clerk may record a receipt but cannot independently authorise a sensitive adjustment.

How different businesses use it

A sole proprietor may begin with simple access; an SME needs delegated responsibility and separation of duties; multi-entity access requires independently qualified boundaries.

Management questions and controls

Inspect privileged actions, failed authorisations, changes to sensitive records, recovery evidence and exceptions requiring independent review.
